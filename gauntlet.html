Pick the bar, not the domain.

In every blind comparison we ran, the better machinery lost to the better bar. This tool spends your two minutes where it counts, then emits a launch prompt under 150 words.

01

Find the axis

Read the verb, not the noun. Teach how attention works → Ciechanowski. Visualise how attention works → Transformer Explainer. Same subject, different ceiling. Getting this backwards is how a 4,000-line system lost to a 120-word prompt.

02

Name the bar

A specific real artifact that is the ceiling on that axis. Not a category.

03

Pick the modality

A screenshot cannot find a behavioural bug. This phrase goes into the prompt verbatim — it's the difference between a critic that checks and one that guesses.

04

Name the hard parts

Not optional. Every emission we tested without a scope line lost coverage — the fleet builds the invoice table beautifully and skips dunning, proration and the empty state. Name the hard parts; never the architecture.

Why this is four steps and not forty.

Across 23 blind judges comparing these prompts against Matt Shumer's original, every decisive difference traced to bar selection or inspection modality. Nothing else reached the emitted prompt, so nothing else changed the outcome. The rest of the system is kept as history at the repo.
