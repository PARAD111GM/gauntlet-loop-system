Write a better Gauntlet Loop.

You've seen the prompt that built a Call of Duty clone. This tool writes you one for whatever you're building — and helps you get the one part right that decides whether it works.

What a Gauntlet Loop is

You tell an AI to build something as good as a specific real thing. It splits the work up, and a second AI that didn't build it compares the result against that real thing and says which is better. If yours loses, it keeps working.

Why most of them fail

Not the wording — the thing you told it to beat. Pick something that's famous but not actually great at the part you care about, and the AI spends hours polishing the wrong thing and still calls it done.

What you'll get

A prompt of about 150 words to paste into Claude Code. Short on purpose. The more you spell out, the less room the AI has to be clever, and the worse the result gets.

01

What are you building?

Write it the way you'd say it out loud. One sentence is plenty — don't describe how it should be built, just what it is.

02

What makes it good?

This is the step everyone skips, and it's the one that decides the whole thing. Two products can be about the same subject and still be good in totally different ways.

Say you're making something about the solar system. If the job is teaching kids how orbits work, the thing to beat is a great explainer video. If the job is letting people fly around and explore, it's NASA's 3D solar system site. Same topic. Aim at the wrong one and you build a beautiful thing nobody wanted.

03

What are you trying to beat?

Name one real, specific thing — the way the original prompt named Call of Duty. Pick something clearly better than you can manage. If you could match it easily, it stops pushing.

04

How should it be checked?

A screenshot can't tell you a button is broken. Pick what you're making and we'll tell the checker how to actually test it, in words it can follow.

05

What's the hard part?

Left alone, an AI builds the easy, obvious bits beautifully and quietly skips the messy ones — the error message, the empty screen, what happens when a card is declined. Name three or four so it can't.

Say what has to work, never how to build it. No frameworks, no libraries.

Then what?

  1. Paste the prompt into Claude Code and let it run. It'll spin up helpers, build, check its work, and go again.
  2. Expect it to take a while and to cost real money. Decide your limit before you start — this loop has no natural finish, which is the whole idea.
  3. It's normal never to actually beat the thing you named. You stop when each new round stops making a real difference. Aiming at something out of reach is what keeps it from stopping early and calling mediocre work done.
